EMERGENCY GUIDE - CHOKING
Document ID: HCC-PUB-004
Date: 2026-02-01
========================================================================
Follows standard first-aid practice (Red Cross / resuscitation council guidance).

SIGNS OF SEVERE CHOKING
- Cannot speak, cry, breathe or cough
- Clutching the throat, face turning blue or grey
- Mild choking: can still cough and speak - encourage coughing, do not slap the back

ADULT OR CHILD OVER 1 YEAR WHO CANNOT BREATHE, COUGH OR SPEAK
1. If they can cough, encourage them to keep coughing.
2. If they cannot cough: stand behind them, lean them forward and give up to 5 firm
   back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. If still choking: give up to 5 abdominal thrusts - fist just above the belly
   button, pull sharply inwards and upwards.
4. Repeat 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts. Call for help / the emergency line.
5. If they become unresponsive, start CPR and get emergency help.

PREGNANT WOMEN AND VERY LARGE ADULTS
Use chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts: stand behind, place your fist on the
middle of the breastbone and pull sharply backwards.

BABY UNDER 1 YEAR
- Lay the baby face down along your forearm, head lower than the body, and give up
  to 5 back blows.
- Then turn the baby face up and give up to 5 chest thrusts with two fingers in the
  centre of the chest. Do NOT use abdominal thrusts on a baby.
- Check the mouth after each round; remove an object only if you can see it.
  Never sweep blindly with a finger.

WHEN YOU CALL FOR HELP, SAY
- "Someone is choking and cannot breathe"
- Age of the person and where you are (nearest landmark)
- What you have done so far

AFTERWARDS
Anyone who received abdominal thrusts must be checked by a health worker afterwards,
because the thrusts can cause internal injury.

PREVENTION FOR SMALL CHILDREN
Cut grapes and sausages lengthways, keep coins, batteries and small toys out of reach,
and always sit with a child who is eating.
