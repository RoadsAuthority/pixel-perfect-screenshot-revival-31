EMERGENCY GUIDE - STROKE AND HEART ATTACK
Document ID: HCC-PUB-007
Date: 2026-02-01
========================================================================
Follows standard first-aid practice.

STROKE - think FAST
- Face: has one side of the face dropped?
- Arms: can they lift both arms and keep them up?
- Speech: is their speech slurred or confused?
- Time: call for emergency help immediately. Note the time the signs started.
Other signs: sudden loss of vision, sudden severe headache, loss of balance.
Do not give food, drink or medicines to someone who may be having a stroke.
Lay them on their side if they are drowsy or vomiting.

Treatment in hospital works best within the first few hours - do not wait to see if
the signs go away.

HEART ATTACK
Signs: chest pain or pressure that may spread to the arm, neck, jaw or back;
shortness of breath; sweating; nausea. Women, older people and people with diabetes
may have only breathlessness, tiredness or upper stomach pain.
1. Call for emergency help immediately.
2. Sit the person down and keep them calm.
3. If they are not allergic and are able to chew, give one adult aspirin (300 mg)
   to chew slowly.
4. If they have their own heart spray (GTN), help them use it.
5. If they become unresponsive and are not breathing normally, start CPR.

CPR (ADULT)
Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, 100-120 times a minute, at least 5 cm
deep. If an automated defibrillator (AED) is available, switch it on and follow the
voice instructions.

PEOPLE AT HIGHER RISK
High blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, high cholesterol and being overweight raise
the risk of stroke and heart attack. Taking chronic medicine every day lowers it.
