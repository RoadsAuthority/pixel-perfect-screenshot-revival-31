EMERGENCY GUIDE - SEVERE BLEEDING
Document ID: HCC-PUB-005
Date: 2026-02-01
========================================================================
Follows standard first-aid practice.

1. Protect yourself: use gloves or a plastic bag over your hand if you can.
2. Press firmly and directly on the wound with a clean cloth or dressing.
3. Keep pressing without stopping for at least 10 minutes. If blood soaks through,
   add more cloth on top - do not remove the first one.
4. If possible, have the person lie down and raise their legs if there is no leg
   injury.
5. Call the emergency line or bring the person to the clinic or hospital.
6. Do not remove objects stuck in a wound; press around them instead.

LIFE-THREATENING BLEEDING FROM AN ARM OR LEG
If firm pressure does not stop blood spurting from an arm or leg, and you are trained,
a tourniquet may be applied 5-7 cm above the wound. Note the time it was applied and
tell the ambulance crew. Never apply a tourniquet to the neck, head or body.

NOSEBLEED
Sit up, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10-15 minutes and breathe
through the mouth. Do not tip the head back. Get help if bleeding lasts more than
20 minutes, or after a head injury.

SIGNS OF SHOCK (URGENT HOSPITAL CARE)
- Pale, cold, sweaty skin
- Fast, weak pulse and fast breathing
- Confusion, drowsiness or fainting
Keep the person lying down and warm with a blanket, and do not give food or drink.

PATIENTS ON BLOOD THINNERS
People who take warfarin or similar medicines bleed for longer. Even small wounds
should be checked at the clinic.
