This is the routine childhood schedule followed at the clinic. It follows the national
Expanded Programme on Immunisation (EPI). Always bring the Road-to-Health booklet.

AGE              VACCINES GIVEN                                          HOW
At birth         BCG (tuberculosis)                                      Injection, right upper arm
                 OPV 0 (oral polio)                                      2 drops by mouth
6 weeks          Pentavalent 1 (diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough,     Injection, left thigh
                 hepatitis B, Hib)
                 OPV 1                                                   2 drops by mouth
                 PCV 1 (pneumococcal)                                    Injection, right thigh
                 Rotavirus 1                                             Drops by mouth
10 weeks         Pentavalent 2, OPV 2, PCV 2, Rotavirus 2                As above
14 weeks         Pentavalent 3, OPV 3, PCV 3, IPV (injectable polio)     As above
9 months         Measles-Rubella 1 (MR1)                                 Injection, right upper arm
18 months        Measles-Rubella 2 (MR2), DTP booster                    Injections
Girls 9-14 years HPV (cervical cancer prevention), 1 dose                Injection, upper arm

VITAMIN A AND DEWORMING
- Vitamin A at 6 months, 12 months and then every 6 months until 5 years
- Deworming (albendazole) every 6 months from 12 months

AFTER VACCINATION - WHAT IS NORMAL
- Mild fever, crying or a sore, red spot where the injection was given for 1-2 days
- A small sore that forms a scar at the BCG site after 2-6 weeks (this is expected)
Give paracetamol syrup by weight if the child has fever, and keep breastfeeding.

COME BACK TO THE CLINIC IF
- Fever lasts more than 2 days, or the child has a fit (convulsion)
- The child is very sleepy, will not feed, or has difficulty breathing
- Swelling of the face or lips, or a widespread rash (allergic reaction)

MISSED A DOSE?
Come to any immunisation day. Missed doses are caught up; the schedule does not
restart. Children up to 5 years can catch up on missed vaccines.
