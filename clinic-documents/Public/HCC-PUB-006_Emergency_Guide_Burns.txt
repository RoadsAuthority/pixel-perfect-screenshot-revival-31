EMERGENCY GUIDE - BURNS
Document ID: HCC-PUB-006
Date: 2026-02-01
========================================================================
Follows standard first-aid practice.

1. Stop the burning: move away from the heat source. If clothes are on fire: stop,
   drop and roll, or smother the flames with a blanket.
2. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes. Cooling
   still helps up to 3 hours after the burn.
3. Remove rings, watches and clothing near the burn unless stuck to the skin.
4. Cover loosely with cling film or a clean plastic bag (lengthways, not wrapped
   tightly around a limb).
5. Do NOT apply ice, butter, oil, toothpaste, egg white or traditional remedies.
6. Do not burst blisters.
7. Keep the person warm - especially small children, who lose heat quickly.

PAIN
Paracetamol or ibuprofen by the dose on the package can be given for pain.

GO TO THE CLINIC OR HOSPITAL IF
- The burn is larger than the person's palm, is deep, or on the face, hands, feet,
  genitals or over a joint
- The person is a child, elderly, pregnant or has diabetes
- The burn is from electricity or chemicals
- The person breathed in smoke (hoarse voice, coughing, soot around the nose)

CHEMICAL BURNS
Brush off dry powder first, then rinse with plenty of running water for at least
20 minutes. Keep the chemical container to show the health worker.

ELECTRICAL BURNS
Switch off the power before touching the person. Electrical burns can damage the
heart even when the skin burn looks small - always go to hospital.

PARAFFIN STOVE AND OPEN FIRE SAFETY
Most burns in children at home come from hot water, paraffin stoves and open fires.
Keep children away from cooking areas and turn pot handles inwards.
