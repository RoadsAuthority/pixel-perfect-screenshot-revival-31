EMERGENCY GUIDE - SNAKEBITE
Document ID: HCC-PUB-008
Date: 2026-02-01
========================================================================
Follows standard first-aid practice for snakebite in southern Africa.

DO
1. Move away from the snake. Keep the person calm and still.
2. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the bite (the limb may swell).
3. Keep the bitten limb still, for example with a splint, at about heart level.
4. Get the person to a hospital quickly - antivenom is only given in hospital.
5. If safe, take a photo of the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it.
6. Note the time of the bite and any changes (swelling, drooping eyelids,
   difficulty swallowing or breathing).

DO NOT
- Cut the wound, suck out venom, or apply ice or electric shocks
- Apply a tight tourniquet
- Give alcohol or traditional remedies

SPITTING COBRA - VENOM IN THE EYES
Rinse the eyes with plenty of water (or milk if no water) for 15 minutes and go to
the clinic. Do not rub the eyes.

DANGER SIGNS ON THE WAY TO HOSPITAL
Drooping eyelids, slurred speech, difficulty breathing or swallowing, bleeding from
the gums, or fast spreading swelling. If breathing stops, start rescue breaths/CPR.

Most snakebites happen at night and in long grass. Wear closed shoes and use a torch
when walking outside after dark.
