PROTOCOL - ANAPHYLAXIS AFTER VACCINATION
Document ID: HCC-CLN-001
Date: 2026-03-01
========================================================================
For trained clinical staff. Based on standard anaphylaxis guidance.
Approved by DOC-001 Dr Anna-Marie van Wyk, 2026-03-01. Review date: 2027-03-01.

RECOGNISE
Sudden onset (usually within 30 minutes) of breathing difficulty, wheeze, swelling of
the lips or throat, widespread rash, collapse or low blood pressure.
Do not confuse with fainting: a faint has a slow pulse, pale skin and quick recovery
when lying flat, with no rash or wheeze.

TREAT
1. Call for help. Lay the patient flat with legs raised (sitting up if breathing is
   difficult; recovery position if unconscious).
2. Give adrenaline 1 mg/mL INTRAMUSCULARLY into the outer thigh:
   0.01 mL/kg (0.01 mg/kg), maximum 0.5 mL (0.5 mg) adult, 0.3 mL child 6-12 years,
   0.15 mL child under 6 years.
3. Repeat after 5 minutes if there is no improvement.
4. Give oxygen if available (15 L/min by mask). Monitor breathing and pulse.
5. Start IV fluids (normal saline 20 mL/kg) if blood pressure is low and a line can
   be placed.
6. Transfer every patient with anaphylaxis to hospital, even after recovery, because
   symptoms can return within 12 hours.

AFTER THE EVENT
- Record the vaccine, batch number, time and treatment in the patient file.
- Report the reaction on the national adverse-event (AEFI) form within 24 hours.
- Write the allergy on the patient's clinic card.
